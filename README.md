# Story Dispatches

Story Dispatches is a web application with several modules that explains the feminine traits of a character often perceived as male, whether it is due to personality or circumstances, using these traits to determine whether it suffices for the character to be actually female. The platform is easy to read and uses media to visualize each character.

## Introduction

Through watching *Zootopia 2* and *Hoppers* in the last few months, I have seen feminine traits in various male characters. For the initial months, I believed Pawbert Lynxley was a girl. His backstory is very remiscent of Cinderella due to the same number of siblings and parents both share, he is very awkward and gentle which is like Cinderella, and his motives were feminized from being the heir to wanting approval which in history is similar to women wanting to belong due to them being treated as objects making it a feminized motives, also females were seen as smaller which makes sense of Pawbert's small size and he was a victim of abuse that is more normally associated with female characters as we see with his encounters with his father. So at a narrative level he stands out, but does he really? Truthfully, even with all that, I never thought of the realistic potential it had, knowing he was already canonically male despite his feminine qualities due to his presentation and voice and how this means announcing he is a girl would be unlikely and how it would not make sense for him to be a female at all. 

After reevaluating Gary De'Snake, I thought about his neutrality that makes him more gender ambiguous and about Nick Wilde's sly personality tying into his usage of "or her" hinting his suspicion Gary isn't really male, as well as tying into how Gary's ancestor is female despite historically women not being founders in contrast to Pawbert coming from a male hierarchy as potential evidence and candidates for a female gender announcement. In addition he is even promoted in female roles in various parodies which would shape his annoouncement, plus, his anti-venom pen is voiced by a female. However despite these circumstances, he lacks feminine qualities aside from his naiveity which is very bog standard and he can be either gender and makes sense either way and in fact is more masculine due to his bravery though that is bog standard and he could still make sense for either gender. 

So does Gary really stand out? No, not really, as we see later on. King George from *Hoppers* may be a potential candidate because in real life beavers are ruled by female colonies and due to his pink radio, but truthfully he still does not stand out because he still comes from a patriarchal lineage, has a mustache, and does not behave in a feminine manner, rather he is more paternal albeit his softness. 

Based on this neither of the three stand out compared to the one thing I realized really stands out which is Titus, a female monarch butterfly who serves as the main antagonist through her sadistic and misanthropic, vengeful scheme to destroy humans through her disguise of herself as a male mayor, a reverse to Mabel Tanaka, the protagonist, disguising herself as a beaver robot, the same mayor Mabel has criticisms against. Titus comes from a female lineage, which is very important because she not only has no father and no siblings present, only a mother, but after both queens meet their demises at respective times, her successor is none other than a female ant rather than a male ant. Ants and beavers do not have as much feminine symbolism as butterflies have throughout human history. So why make the ant female if King George is male? It only reflects on Titus' part and how she is even less male than the ant who is less male than George. Not only that, but in the English dub, while females do and have voiced young boys and do have similar pitches, she talks like a girl more distinctively rather than a generic child, unlike Darla Sherman in *Finding Nemo*, an 8 year old girl. Not only that but she struggles like a typical woman would with male disguise. When she disguises herself as the male mayor, she is disgusted by the tie because she is uncomfortable with masculinity while acting like its because she did not know humans wear ties, even making remarks that are disgusting against the tie. This shows Titus does not make sense as male and that the Insect Prince and Insect Kings are false titles, solidifying her as the female character misperceived or mispromoted as male by the audience. Through this, she and Gary stand out for a potential gender announcement, but Gary fails massively to Titus due to him making sense of as either gender. This determines through all this why Titus fits better than the other characters.

The goal of Story Dispatches is clear up any misinformation and organize all these thoughts into five modules, explaining the potential female traits for each character, how they stand out in their own way, or whether they stand out as a whole.

## Glossary
It has five modules: Pawbert Lynxley and Gary De'Snake from *Zootopia 2*, King George and Titus from *Hoppers*, and a module that determines why Titus is the only case that is really valid.

- **Pawbert Lynxley**: Module for Pawbert Lynxley and their feminine qualities
- **Gary De'Snake**: Module for Gary De'Snake and their feminine qualities
- **King George**: Module for King George and their feminine qualities
- **Titus**: Module for Titus and their feminine qualities
- **Why Titus Holds And The Others Do Not**: Explanation on why Titus stands out compared to the others
